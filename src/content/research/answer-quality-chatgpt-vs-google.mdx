A pilot study. <mark>25 prompts</mark> across different verticals, three
engines, blinded and ranked by an independent judge (Opus 5).

## Summary

| Engine | Mean overall rank | Firsts |
|:--|--:|--:|
| ChatGPT | <mark>1.20</mark> | 21 |
| AI Overview | 2.28 | 1 |
| AI Mode | 2.52 | 3 |

- ChatGPT first on <mark>21 of 25</mark>. The two Google surfaces trade second and third
  round to round — the gap between *them* is not stable.

## Method

<Callout>
  <p>This is a pilot study, sized to see whether the effect is worth measuring
  properly. 25 prompts were sampled at random from a single crawl on 2026-08-27,
  keeping only those where all three engines answered without error. For each
  prompt the three answers were relabelled <code>SOURCE-A</code>,
  <code>SOURCE-B</code> and <code>SOURCE-C</code> under a fresh shuffle, so the
  same letter is a different engine every time. A judge who never saw the key
  ranked them first, second and third, checking factual claims against live web
  search.</p>
  <p>Every answer was captured signed out, on each engine's free public surface —
  no accounts, no subscriptions, no personalisation or chat history.</p>
</Callout>

## Takeaways

### ChatGPT wins by committing

It is the engine that picks a brand, quotes a number, gives a handle that
resolves, and names the constraint that actually decides the question — then
stops. The Google surfaces more often assemble competent analysis and decline to
land it: they lost q16, q19, q2, q7 and q10 by offering to continue instead of
answering. Being <mark>specific</mark> and sometimes wrong beat being thorough
and non-committal.

### Citation count runs inverse to answer quality

Winning answers carried a mean of <mark>5.0 citations</mark>; second place 8.2
and third 8.5. The winner had the fewest citations of the three on 10 of 25
prompts, and the most on just 3. AI Mode cited 23 publishers on q13 — including
a library-discovery platform and a job board — and finished last. Volume is not
the signal.

### The best answers are often not the longest

If anything the data leans the other way. Winning answers averaged <mark>4,259
characters</mark> against 4,914 for second and 5,121 for third, and the winner
was the shortest of the three on 13 of 25 prompts — q6 won on 2,317 characters
against 5,982. The correlation between length and rank is weak (Spearman +0.10),
so read this as *length does not buy quality* rather than as brevity winning on
its own. Longer reads as more thorough and scores as more padded.

### Where the answer *is* the identifier, omitting it is fatal

Asked for Telegram channels, only ChatGPT gave <mark>handles that resolve</mark>;
the Google arms named channels the user cannot find. Same shape as the withheld
affiliate commission rates. The useful unit is a resolvable name, not a
description.

### Firing on a prompt you should decline is worse than declining

q12 had no antecedent; AI Overview invented a scenario and shipped it with
<mark>7 citations</mark> while both others asked for context. Citations on a
hallucinated premise make the error harder to catch, not easier.

### Most failure modes are not hallucinations or fabrications

Across 25 queries the errors were overwhelmingly <mark>stale or
mischaracterised</mark> — a real company described wrongly, a real product with
last year's terms, a rebrand not yet absorbed — rather than invented. That
matters for how you correct them: the fix is fresher public information, not
catching a model making things up. The exception: q7 produced four
unreproducible fund return figures (26.46% claimed against 23.79% published), in
the one vertical where a wrong number costs money directly.

### Rebrands lag on the Google surfaces

Only ChatGPT knew Velocity Global → Pebl (q5). All three still said "LambdaTest"
<mark>8 months</mark> after the TestMu AI rebrand (q13). Budget many months for a
rename to reach these surfaces.

### What all three miss

For every prompt the judge also recorded what none of the engines said. Those
gaps are the opening: if no answer engine surfaces a fact, no competitor is being
cited for it either. Three examples from this set:

- Over a 7-year hold, Indian capital-gains tax and the ~1 percentage point a year
  cost of a regular rather than direct plan matter more than the gap between the
  funds being compared. None of the three raised either (q7).
- Suno's free tier stops allowing downloads on 3 September 2026, a week after we
  captured. All three recommended it; <mark>none mentioned</mark> the cap (q9).
- Amazon runs its own vetted Service Provider Network directory, the obvious
  place to check an agency before hiring one. No engine pointed to it (q6).

## Study conditions and limitations

- A pilot study, not a benchmark: n=25 prompts, all captured from one geography
  — a US locale, with the Google arms pinned to New York. Treat the direction as
  the finding and the exact percentages as provisional.
- Signed-out and free tiers only. Every capture was anonymous, so none of this
  speaks to what a paid or logged-in user sees — ChatGPT Plus, Google AI Pro, or
  any surface with personalisation and chat history could rank differently.
- Judged by Claude Opus 5. Cross-checked on a sample with a second judge
  (`gemini-3.7-flash`, temp 0, n=8) against the same blinded files and the same
  per-query rubric — <mark>seven of eight</mark> exact agreement, the lone disagreement being
  a query two judges already contest. The second judge is one of Google's own
  models, and it ranked Google's own surfaces second and third on every query it
  saw, so the result is not an artifact of same-family preference in the judge.
  Five of the eight ran with live web search, and reproduced the first judge's
  order on all five including a second/third swap.
- Declining to answer counts against a source. AI Mode and AI Overview lost q16,
  q19 and q2 by refusing to conclude after competent analysis. A reader who
  thinks a search surface *should* stay neutral would rank those differently.
- Judges disagree on facts, not just taste — two judges searched live on q20 and
  reached opposite ground truth. Truthfulness ranks are softer than they look.
- The other 17 scored queries are single-judge.
